How the money is worked out

Running costs: what it costs to keep a shop open for an hour: staff, power and upkeep. Shutting an hour saves this.

Sales profit: what the sales in that hour earn after the cost of what was sold. Shutting an hour gives this up.

Better off is running costs saved minus sales profit given up. When it is above zero, shutting that hour makes money.

Break-even: the takings an hour needs for its sales profit to cover its running costs. Below it, the hour loses money.

Closing time: shops open as planned, so the only choice is when to close, or whether to stay shut. The call picks the closing time that earns the most across the whole day, so a quiet morning can be carried by a lunchtime rush that follows. Changes worth only a few pounds are left alone.

Allow for an upturn: the forecast can be wrong, and a good day pays. This setting makes the call harder to close early: it only closes when that still pays if sales beat the forecast by the amount you choose.

Predicted: each hour’s takings are the usual for that weekday, adjusted for the weather. The usual already includes each shop’s normal busy spells, such as lunch.

Be careful: a dark unit makes the Pier feel quiet, and costs and margins here are sample figures. A manager should approve every call.

How this works

The money: every decision here is a money decision. Shutting saves the shop’s running costs for the rest of the day and gives up the sales profit it would have made. The amount better off is the difference.

Demo replay: a past day’s takings are played back as if it were happening now. At the Pier the same board would be fed by the tills, and the check would run on the hour from the hourly sales figures.

The hourly check: each shop that has traded a full hour is judged on that hour. It needs a decision only if it is under your line, quieter than that shop usually is at that hour, and shutting would save a worthwhile amount over the rest of the day. A slow opening or a normal lull does not raise a false alarm.

The saving: what shutting now would save over the rest of the day, if the shop follows its usual shape at today’s level, lunch rush included. With an allowance for an upturn set, it only counts when it still pays if the rest of the day sells that much better. It leaves out the knock-on effect of a dark unit, so the decision stays with a manager.
